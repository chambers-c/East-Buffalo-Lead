Lead & health

Information helps
protect families.

Lead exposure is preventable. Understanding where it may be found can help you ask questions, arrange testing, and connect with support.

Health note: There is no known safe level of lead exposure for children. This page shares general educational information, not a diagnosis. Talk with a health care provider about concerns or testing for a child.
Why awareness matters

Possible effects
of lead exposure

CHILDREN

Growing brains and bodies

Lead can harm a child’s developing brain and nervous system. Exposure may affect learning, attention, behavior, growth, and hearing.

PREGNANCY

Added care matters

Lead exposure can be harmful during pregnancy. A health professional can discuss individual concerns and appropriate testing.

ADULTS

Health can be affected

Lead can also affect adults. If you think you may have been exposed, ask a health care provider about your situation.

Where might lead come from?

Potential sources can include lead-based paint and household dust in older housing, contaminated soil, and plumbing or fixtures that contain lead. Testing and a qualified assessment can help identify what needs attention.

Everyday precautions

Steps you can take
while you seek help

These steps can reduce contact with some common sources. They do not replace testing or professional advice.

At home

  • Wash children’s hands and toys often, especially before meals.
  • Wet-wipe floors and window areas to reduce dust; avoid dry sweeping paint dust.
  • Keep children away from peeling paint and home repair areas that create dust.
  • Use cold tap water for drinking and cooking; follow current local guidance about flushing and filters.

Make a plan

  • Ask your child’s health care provider about blood lead testing.
  • Contact Buffalo Water to ask about lead water testing.
  • Contact LeadSAFE Erie County about housing assessments and prevention instruction.
  • Before renovations, ask a qualified professional about lead-safe work practices.
